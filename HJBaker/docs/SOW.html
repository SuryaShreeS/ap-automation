Section 2

Background and what success looks like

Blue Creek holds the image, routes for approval and lets one traveling approver approve by email. The keying, coding and matching all happen afterwards in Sage. That gives five specific problems — manual coding as the dominant cost, a 500-line ceiling that pushes big load lists out of the tool entirely, hourly file-based integration with a 90-day window, a settlement chain that cannot record its third link, and no operational visibility.

PRD §1 states these in full with the measures attached. The commercial case rests on the first one: coding is where the hours go, and lines that arrive already coded are hours that do not have to be worked.

Section 4

Solution architecture, in summary

Full detail is in PRD §15. What matters commercially is that the platform is a small number of components with one integration boundary, and that infrastructure is defined as code — which is precisely what makes the two hosting options in Section 9 interchangeable rather than a fork in the build.

The same platform, two places it can run

In both options Sage X3 remains at H.J. Baker and the platform reaches it over a private, authenticated connection. Neither option requires Sage to be exposed publicly.

Section 5

Delivery approach and plan

Twenty-four weeks from kickoff to the first company live, then a staged rollout and four weeks of hypercare. Work is delivered in two-week increments with a working system demonstrated at the end of each one — the same way the prototype was built, so there is no long silence between kickoff and something you can look at.

Cutover is company by company, not big bang, and the automation threshold starts high and rises as measured accuracy earns it (PRD §16).

What the estimate assumes

  • Sage X3 exposes the required reads and document creation through a supported API, and Net at Work is available to confirm the contract in week 1 to 3 (decision D-01). A file-based fallback instead of live APIs would add approximately 25 person-days.
  • Extraction uses a managed document-intelligence service with a pre-trained invoice model. Training custom models per vendor layout is not included; if a material number of vendors need it, allow 1 to 2 days each.
  • Configuration data — approval hierarchy, limits, templates, company mapping — is provided by H.J. Baker as data, not derived by Alferix from documents.
  • One production Sage instance and one test instance, both reachable from the platform environments.
  • English-language documents; seven companies; four currencies; roughly 2,700 vendors.
  • Volumes per PRD §5: approximately 3,300 invoices a month, peaking near 3,000 additional documents in a quarterly true-up window.
  • Estimate confidence is ±10% at this stage, narrowing to ±5% at the end of W1. Movement beyond that is handled through change control (Section 11).

Section 10

Assumptions

Stated so that if one turns out to be wrong, the consequence is visible rather than argued about later.

  • Sage X3 exposes the required reads and document creation through a supported API. A file-based fallback adds approximately 25 person-days (Section 7).
  • Sage remains the system of record. The platform does not replicate the general ledger and does not become a reporting source for statutory accounts.
  • Extraction uses a managed document-intelligence service with a pre-trained invoice model; per-vendor custom models are not included.
  • Volumes are approximately 3,300 invoices a month with true-up peaks near 3,000 additional documents, per PRD §5. A sustained doubling would require re-sizing, which affects infrastructure cost but not the build.
  • Seven operating companies, four currencies, English-language documents, approximately 2,700 vendors, approximately 60 platform users.
  • Approval hierarchy and limits exist as business rules that can be expressed as data.
  • Two environments on the client side: one Sage test instance and one production.
  • Client resources are available to the levels in Section 9 during the periods stated.
  • Work is delivered remotely, with on-site presence for kickoff, UAT preparation and go-live if requested — travel charged at cost against prior approval.
  • Documents and configuration are supplied in the formats already shared (pipe-delimited exports, PDF samples).
  • Scope is as Section 3. Anything not listed there is a change (Section 11).

Section 11

Acceptance and change control

Acceptance

  • Each milestone in Section 5 is accepted against the criteria stated there and, for the five flows, against the acceptance criteria in the corresponding PRD section.
  • H.J. Baker has five working days from delivery to accept or to state specific deficiencies in writing. Absent either, the milestone is deemed accepted.
  • Deficiencies are classified by severity. Severity 1 and 2 defects block acceptance; severity 3 and 4 are logged and fixed in a subsequent increment without blocking.
  • A milestone is not rejected for items outside its stated criteria.
  • Warranty: defects against this SOW and the PRD reported within 90 days of go-live are corrected at no charge. This is not a substitute for support, which covers operational matters, environment issues and user questions.

Change control

  • Any change to scope, the PRD, the plan or the dependencies is raised as a change request stating the change, the effort in person-days, the schedule impact and the price impact.
  • No change is implemented until approved in writing by both the H.J. Baker business owner and the Alferix engagement lead.
  • Changes under 2 person-days are absorbed within the increment at no charge, up to 10 person-days across the engagement — so small course corrections do not need paperwork.
  • Decisions in PRD §17 answered differently from the assumption stated there are handled as changes, with D-02 (universal versus rule-driven AP review) the one most likely to matter.
  • The 40-hour monthly enhancement bucket in Option 2, and the included hours in the Option 1 retainer, absorb post-go-live changes without a change request.
