Section 1

Purpose, problem and objectives

H.J. Baker processes accounts payable for seven operating companies through a single mailbox and a single ERP. The current tool, Blue Creek, does three useful things: it holds the image, it routes for approval, and it lets one traveling approver approve from email. Everything else — the keying, the coding, the matching — happens in Sage afterwards. In Symantha's words, "all the keying, the coding, all of that resides in Sage."

That leaves five specific problems, and they are the reasons this platform exists.

Problems this platform must solve
ProblemTodayRequired outcome
Manual coding Every line is keyed by hand into Sage after approval. Named as the single biggest cost in the process. Lines arrive coded — from the purchase order, from a receipt, or from a standing template — and a person edits exceptions rather than typing everything.
The 500-line ceiling Blue Creek cannot handle more than 500 lines. A thousand-line load list has to be worked outside the tool entirely. No line-count limit. A document with 1,000+ lines across several purchase orders is processed whole (NFR-2).
Batch-and-wait integration Pipe-delimited files over SFTP, hourly, with a rolling 90-day window. Anything older than 90 days is invisible to the tool. Live Sage reads at the moment of use and a synchronous write with the document number returned (FR-82, FR-13).
The broken third link An original invoice can be linked to its credit memo, but the rebill that settles it cannot be linked to either. "You lose that third link." Original invoice, credit memo and rebill are one provable chain with all three Sage numbers on it (FR-62).
No operational visibility Volume, auto-match rate, PO versus non-PO split and vendor breakdown are not readily available. Dashboards on live data where every number is clickable through to the exact documents behind it (§13).

Section 2

Scope

The platform owns the document from the moment it arrives to the moment Sage confirms payment. Sage remains the system of record for the ledger; the platform never becomes a second set of books.

In scope

  • Intake from the AP mailbox, including multi-attachment emails and scanned batches.
  • Extraction of header and line data with per-field confidence.
  • Rule-based classification into five document types, with a human queue below the confidence threshold.
  • The five processing flows in full, including every gate where a person must decide.
  • Two- and three-way matching against Sage purchase orders and receipts, at receipt level.
  • Line coding from purchase orders, receipts and standing templates.
  • Approval hierarchy, ad-hoc routing, limits, delegation and email approval.
  • AP final review as a rule-driven control point.
  • Creation of purchase invoices and credit memos in Sage with the image attached, and capture of the returned document number.
  • Payment status feedback from Sage.
  • Prepayment positions and drawdown; credit and rebill settlement chains.
  • Search, dashboards, analytics and a complete audit trail.
  • Administration of vendors' routing behaviour, templates, reference lists and users.

Out of scope

  • Purchase order creation. Confirmed as not automatable — orders originate at manual ordering sites and from IRT rail and truck line imports. The platform asks for the order number; it does not invent one (see §17, retired requirement).
  • Changes inside Sage X3 itself, Sage licensing, and any Sage-side development. Where Sage work is needed it is carried out by Net at Work.
  • Purchase requisitioning, goods receipting, inventory and the general ledger.
  • Payment execution and bank file generation — Sage does this.
  • Vendor self-service portal, vendor onboarding workflow and vendor statement reconciliation.
  • Accounts receivable, expenses, payroll and any non-AP document.
  • Statutory e-invoicing network connectivity (Peppol and similar). Not required in the current territories; noted as a future consideration.
  • Handwriting recognition beyond the printed and stamped content of freight tickets. Illegible documents are routed to a person by design, not by failure.

Section 3

Users, roles and authority

Six kinds of person touch a document, and the difference between them is authority, not seniority. The platform enforces authority; it does not ask users to remember it.

Roles
RoleDoesCannot
Processor / coder Works the queues: resolves extraction exceptions, attaches purchase orders, codes lines, submits for approval. Approve above their own limit, or approve a document they submitted.
Approver Approves within a limit, by company. May approve from email. Edit coding after approval without the document returning to the processor.
AP team Works exceptions, releases documents held by an AP rule, creates credit memos, manages park queues. Bypass the approval hierarchy.
AP administrator Symantha's role. Full configuration: routing rules, thresholds, templates, reference lists, users, limits. Investigates why a document will not match. Alter a posted Sage document — that is a Sage action.
Treasury / CFO Approves prepayments and large commitments; watches prepaid balances. —
IT administrator Manages access, integration credentials and environment configuration. See or alter financial content beyond what their AP role grants.

Authority and escalation (FR-40 – FR-45)

  • Every approver has a limit per company. A document above the limit escalates up the hierarchy automatically; the user does not choose the next approver (FR-41).
  • Vendors on the hierarchy list route by hierarchy. Everything else routes ad-hoc, to the person the business names. The list is client-owned and maintained by the AP team (FR-42).
  • Approval is required even when matching was automatic — "if it's matched on an auto match, it still has to go through the approval process" — unless the AP team has explicitly exempted that vendor or condition (FR-43).
  • Nobody approves their own submission. Enforced, not advised (FR-44).
  • Delegation is time-boxed and audited, and the original approver remains visible on the record (FR-45).

Section 4

Operating companies and company determination

Each document belongs to exactly one of seven companies, and the vendor rarely states which. It follows from who the invoice is sold to, and that single fact then drives the accounting entity, the tax treatment, the currency, the approval route and every report the business looks at. Get it wrong and everything downstream is wrong.

Operating companies

435HJB Sulphur UKUnited Kingdom entityGBP · VAT

FR-05 — company determination. The platform maps the sold-to name on the document to a company code through a client-owned mapping list. When a new trading name appears, that list is the single place it is added. If no mapping exists and the document does not state a company, the document is held for a person with the reason stated; it is never guessed.

A material share of activity is intercompany — Canadian entities in particular appear on both sides of settlement arrangements. Intercompany documents follow the same five flows; the company pair is recorded so intercompany reporting and elimination remain possible.

Write — platform to Sage (FR-13)

One call per document creates the purchase invoice or credit memo with the image attached and returns the document number, which is written back onto the platform record immediately. This replaces both the hourly file drop and the 90-day lookback limit. The call carries: company, vendor and address ID, invoice number and date, due date, the coding block per line, line amounts and descriptions, net, tax, freight, gross, currency, the PO or non-PO flag, and the platform ID that lets Sage and the platform find each other later.

Writes are idempotent on the platform ID: a retry after a timeout cannot create a second purchase invoice (NFR-8).

Section 6

Intake, extraction and classification

One route in, one classification decision, five destinations. Everything that follows in sections 7 to 11 assumes this has already happened.

Intake requirements

Intake — step by step
RefStepControl
FR-10Poll the AP mailbox and ingest every attachment as its own document, preserving the source email for later reading. Nothing is ingested twice; the email remains viewable from the document.
FR-11Accept PDF, TIFF and common image formats, single or multi-page, including scanned batches of several invoices in one file. Batch scans are separated; each separated document is auditable back to the original file and page range.
FR-12Refuse duplicates on the combination of vendor, invoice number and amount. Catches genuine resends without blocking legitimate re-invoicing at a different amount. The refusal names the document already holding the number.
FR-20Extract header fields and every line, with a confidence score per field. No line-count ceiling. Fields below a per-field threshold are marked for the processor rather than silently accepted.
FR-22Read the vendor record live from Sage at extraction time. Terms, currency, pay method and remit detail always reflect Sage.
FR-25Show the image beside the extracted data with each field's confidence visible. A processor can verify a value against the document without leaving the screen.

Classification rules (FR-30, FR-33)

Rules are evaluated in priority order and the first match wins. The table is configuration, not code — the AP administrator adds, reorders, disables and edits rules, and changes are audited.

Routing rules in priority order
PriorityConditionRoutes toWhy this order
10Vendor is marked as prepaidPrepayments The vendor relationship overrides document appearance. "We could mark those two vendors — anything from them is a prepayment."
20Negative total, or cancellation wordingCredits A negative total is unambiguous and must not be posted as an invoice.
32Vendor is marked as a settlement (netback) vendor and the document re-invoices at a settled priceRebills Only these vendors produce rebills; the marking lives on the supplier profile.
40A purchase order number is present and validPO invoices The strongest positive signal available.
99Anything elseNon-PO invoices The safe default: a person will decide whether an order is needed.

The confidence threshold. A single configurable threshold (default 0.80) decides whether the platform routes on its own or asks. Below it the document waits in Manual Review with the reason stated — poor scan, unknown vendor, ambiguous document type — and a person classifies it in a screen that shows the image on one side and the five types on the other. Selecting a type only selects it; a confirm action commits and moves to the next document, so a mis-click costs nothing.

An unknown vendor applies a fixed penalty to confidence, because a document from a vendor Sage has never seen is exactly the case where a confident-looking guess is most dangerous.

Rollout posture. Automatic routing is earned, not assumed. The threshold starts high, and the business raises automation company by company as accuracy proves itself — which is how Symantha asked for it.

Manual review edge cases

The scan is poor or the vendor name is unreadable
Held for a person with the reason stated rather than guessed at. A faxed handwritten ticket and a degraded scan end up in the same place.
The document is a statement of account, not an invoice
Held for a person. A statement listing several open items is not something to post, and the ambiguity is surfaced rather than resolved silently.
One email carries several invoices
Split into separate documents at intake, each classified on its own merits, all traceable to the one email.
The document is not an AP document at all
A person marks it as out of scope with a reason; it leaves the working queues but stays searchable and auditable.

Process steps

PO invoice — required behaviour, step by step
StepWhat happensActorControl enforced

Prepayments — required behaviour, step by step

Section 11 · Rebills

Settlement vendors invoice provisionally, then settle at a final price — month-end, in the second month, or reaching back three months. The provisional invoice is credited and the goods re-invoiced at the settled price. The rebill's job is to close a three-document chain, and today that chain breaks.

Recognised by
A settlement vendor re-invoicing at a settled price
Owned by
Processor, approver, AP for closed periods
Becomes in Sage
Purchase invoice linked to the credit memo
Stops for a person
The credit memo reference, approval, and AP if the period is closed
The static field. This is the gap the business named directly: "we can link the original PIN to the credit memo, but we can't currently link the rebill to either one — you lose that third link." The platform carries a dedicated field on the rebill holding the purchase credit memo it settles, so original invoice, credit and rebill are provable in one place (FR-62). It is a gate: no reference, no chain.

2The purchase credit memo this rebill settles is captured in a dedicated field and validated. ProcessorGate. Format enforced as PCM plus six digits; no reference means no chain (FR-62).

4Matching runs against the purchase order as for any PO invoice. PlatformSettled price replaces provisional; the difference is visible.
5Approval routes by hierarchy and limit. ApproverStandard controls.
6If the settlement reaches into a closed accounting period, the document routes to AP with that condition stated. AP teamThe platform does not decide period questions; it presents them (FR-64).
7The purchase invoice is created in Sage and the returned PIN completes the chain. PlatformChain integrity is reportable: complete chains versus missing links.

Acceptance criteria

  • A rebill cannot link its chain until the credit memo it settles is recorded.
  • A completed chain shows the original PIN, the PCM and the rebill PIN together.
  • An incomplete chain states exactly which link is missing.
  • A closed-period rebill appears in AP review with the condition named.
  • Chain integrity is reportable across all rebills, not only inspectable one at a time.

Section 12

Cross-cutting logic and controls

Behaviour that applies to every document type, regardless of which flow it is in.

Matching (FR-30 – FR-36)

  • Match type is taken from the purchase-order line, not decided by the platform. Sage carries 2-Way or 3-Way per line.
  • Three-way matching is at receipt level, and the unique key for a receipt is the ship date and BOL pair — this is what makes a thousand-receipt monthly summary tractable.
  • Quantity and price tolerances are configuration, set per company and optionally per vendor.
  • A partial receipt matches partially, and the remaining quantity stays visible on the order rather than closing it.
  • Unit-of-measure conversions on the order (pounds to long tons, for example) are applied from Sage's own conversion, never recalculated locally.

Coding and templates (FR-26, FR-28)

  • Matched lines are coded from the purchase order. Unmatched lines are coded from a standing template, or by hand as a last resort.
  • Templates are built and maintained by the AP team, not by developers. A template carries the full coding block and a description; a processor picks one, enters the amount, and submits.
  • Every coding value is validated against the live Sage reference lists at submission, so a code retired in Sage cannot be posted from here.

AP final review (FR-44)

AP final review is a rule-driven control point. The rules are configuration and start from what the business already does today: amount above a threshold, a closed accounting period, every credit, a new vendor's first invoice, a variance released by a processor, and any vendor the AP team names.

Open decision D-02. In session 002 the business stated "everything has to hit this final AP review before it comes into Sage." The platform is built rule-driven rather than universal, because universal review makes AP a bottleneck on every document and removes most of the benefit. Both are one configuration change apart. This needs an explicit business decision before UAT — see §17.

Duplicate and integrity controls

  • Duplicates are refused on vendor, invoice number and amount together (FR-12).
  • A purchase order already consumed by another invoice cannot be attached again, and the holder is named.
  • Net plus taxes plus freight must equal gross before submission (FR-27).
  • Writes to Sage are idempotent on platform ID; a timeout retry cannot create a second document.
  • Nothing is deleted. Cancellation and out-of-scope marking are states with reasons, and the record remains searchable.

Search and the click-through contract (FR-70 – FR-72)

Search covers header fields with exact, wildcard and range operators, and line-level fields where the business needs them — account, division, project, site.

Every count displayed anywhere in the platform — a dashboard tile, a queue badge, a sub-queue chip — carries the exact filter that produced it, and clicking it lands on those documents. This is a hard contract, not a convention: a count you cannot click through to is a defect. In the prototype it is enforced by 539 automated invariant checks on every displayed number.

Audit trail (FR-46, FR-83)

  • Every state change, field edit, approval, rejection, release and Sage write is recorded with timestamp, actor and message.
  • The document's own history is readable from the document: when it arrived, when it was classified and at what confidence, every processing step, who approved it, which Sage numbers were created and when payment was confirmed.
  • Configuration changes — routing rules, thresholds, limits, templates, users — are audited with before and after values.
  • Audit records are append-only and retained per the retention policy (NFR-11).

Section 15

Architecture

Four layers, one integration boundary, and a deliberately small number of moving parts. Sage X3 stays the system of record; the platform is where documents are worked before they become Sage documents.

Layers and responsibilities

Layers
LayerHoldsNever does
Web applicationQueues, document detail, search, dashboards, administration. Server-rendered application shell with a typed API client. Business rules. The UI asks; it does not decide.
API and servicesIntake, extraction orchestration, classification, the engine, approvals, search, reporting. Hold state in memory between requests.
Processing engineThe five flows as declared step machines: each step knows its gate, its precondition, its action and its audit marker. Encode a flow in scattered conditionals. A flow is data, so it is inspectable and testable.
Sage adapterEvery call to Sage, behind one typed interface: reference lists, vendors, orders, receipts, document creation, validation, payments. Leak Sage-specific detail into the engine.
DataRelational operational store for documents, lines, positions, chains, rules, users and audit. Object store for images. Duplicate the general ledger.

Why the engine is data-driven

Each document type's flow is a declared list of steps. A step states whether it is a human gate, what makes it complete, what it does when it runs, and what it records. The canonical state of a document is its status plus its audit markers — never a hidden variable — so the same flow definition drives the queues, the status display, the dashboards and the tests.

The practical consequence: adding a control (the rebill's credit-memo gate, for example) is one step declaration plus one endpoint, and every screen and count that depends on it updates without change. That is what keeps a platform with this many exceptions maintainable.

Integration patterns

  • Reads on use. Vendor, order, receipt and reference data are read at the moment they are needed, so the platform never shows a stale copy. Reference lists are cached with a short life and refreshed daily.
  • Synchronous write with confirmation. Document creation is a single call that returns the Sage number, which is written onto the record immediately.
  • Idempotency. Every write carries the platform ID as its key. Sage's own validation feed already carries that ID today, which is what makes this safe.
  • Bounded retry and dead-letter. Failures retry with backoff, then surface as a visible state with the error — never a silent loss.
  • Daily reconciliation. A scheduled job compares platform records against Sage documents and reports any divergence. This is the control that catches the failure nobody noticed.

Environments

  • Development — Alferix-side, mock Sage adapter.
  • Test / UAT — connected to a Sage X3 test instance with representative data. Client-accessible.
  • Production — connected to Sage X3 production. Access restricted; all changes released through the pipeline.

Infrastructure is defined as code so all three are provisioned identically, which is also what makes the two hosting options in the SOW genuinely interchangeable.

Retired requirement

Automatic purchase-order creation — retired. An earlier requirement had the platform generate the next purchase order. The sessions established this is not automatable: orders originate at manual ordering sites and from IRT rail and truck line imports, with quantities and prices set by people. The platform therefore asks for the order number and shows the vendor's last order for reference. Retiring this removed a real risk of creating orders nobody asked for.
